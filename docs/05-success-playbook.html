1. Start Here: The Truth About Getting Customers

A website does not make the phone ring. A website is like a business card. It helps people trust you after they find you. Customers find you because you go out and get in front of them, every day.

Where handyman customers actually come from (biggest first):

  1. People who already know you (friends, family, old coworkers, neighbors)
  2. Google Maps (Google Business Profile with lots of reviews)
  3. Referrals from happy customers
  4. Nextdoor and Facebook neighborhood groups
  5. Partners who send you work (realtors, property managers, other trades)
  6. Showing up in neighborhoods (yard signs, door hangers near your jobs, truck magnet)
  7. Lead apps (Thumbtack, Angi, TaskRabbit). These cost money but can fill gaps early.

The one rule that matters most: Do 10 "customer touches" every single day until your calendar is full. A touch is anything that puts you in front of a possible customer: a text to someone you know, a post, a comment answering "need a handyman?", a door hanger, a realtor visit, a review request. Track them with tally marks. 10 a day = 300 a month. That fills a calendar.

Expect this timeline: Weeks 1 to 4 feel slow. Months 2 to 3 pick up as reviews come in. By month 6, referrals start doing the work for you. Most people quit in month 1. Don't.

2. Professional Standards

Customers are letting a stranger into their home. They decide in the first 10 seconds whether they trust you. It's based on how you look, how you smell, and how you talk. Fair or not, this decides if you get hired again and if they tell their friends. Every pro (plumbers, nurses, realtors) follows rules like these.

2A. Daily Hygiene Checklist (every work day, no exceptions)

  • ☐ Shower every morning before work (use soap, wash hair)
  • ☐ Deodorant every morning. Keep a second deodorant in the truck and put more on at lunch, especially in Austin heat.
  • ☐ Brush teeth. Keep gum or mints in the truck. No strong food (onions, garlic) before a job if you can avoid it.
  • ☐ Beard trimmed and neat, hair under control (the cap helps)
  • ☐ Clean hands and fingernails (scrub brush by the sink at home)
  • ☐ No smoke or vape smell. If you smoke or vape, don't do it in the truck or before entering a home. Customers smell it immediately.
  • ☐ No strong cologne either. "Clean" is the goal, not "perfume."

2B. Clothes

Your clothes don't have to be brand new, but they have to look reasonably clean and smell clean.

  • ☐ Start every day in a fresh shirt (branded shirt or plain solid color). Never re-wear a work shirt without washing it.
  • ☐ Work pants or jeans with no big holes or stains
  • ☐ Clean-ish boots. Knock the mud off before every job.
  • ☐ Keep a spare shirt in the truck. If you sweat through or get dirty on job 1, change before job 2.
  • ☐ Do laundry twice a week (put it on your calendar: Wednesday and Sunday)

The "Smell Check": Before you walk into any home, stop at the truck and check: shirt, armpits, breath. If anything is off: deodorant, mints, spare shirt. Takes 30 seconds.

2C. The Truck Is Part of Your Look

  • ☐ No trash, fast food bags, or bottles visible in the cab
  • ☐ Tools organized in bins, not a pile
  • ☐ Car wash once a month (customers see it in their driveway and neighbors see the magnet)

2D. Behavior in a Customer's Home

  1. Knock or ring, then step back from the door. Smile.
  2. Shoe covers on before you step inside, every time.
  3. Phone in your pocket. No personal calls, no scrolling, no speakerphone in their house.
  4. No swearing. No talk about politics, religion, or other customers.
  5. Ask before using their bathroom. Leave it cleaner than you found it.
  6. Don't eat, smoke, or vape on their property. Eat in the truck.
  7. No loud music. If you want music, use one earbud, low.
  8. Don't open closets, drawers, or rooms you weren't asked into.
  9. If you're working with kids or pets around, keep doors closed and tools out of reach.
  10. Before leaving: vacuum, wipe surfaces, take ALL trash and packaging with you.

2E. Communication Rules

  • Reply to every message within 1 hour during the work day (8 AM to 6 PM).
  • Use full sentences. Say hi and use their name. No "k," "ya," or "lol" with customers.
  • Never say "I'll be there around 10ish." Say "I'll be there at 10:00." If you're going to be late, text before, not after.
  • Never badmouth another handyman or a customer's old work. Say "Let's get this fixed right."
  • If you don't know how to do something, say: "Let me look into the best way to do that and get back to you today." Then actually get back today.

3. Time Management System

Being on time is the #1 thing customers judge. This system takes the guessing out of it. Your phone does the remembering so your brain doesn't have to.

3A. One Calendar for Everything

  • Use Google Calendar on your phone. Everything goes in it: jobs, quotes to send, laundry, gym, family stuff.
  • Rule: If it's not in the calendar, it doesn't exist. The moment someone books, put it in before you hang up.
  • Every job entry includes: customer name, phone, address, the list, price, materials. (Then you never have to dig through texts.)
  • Turn on 2 notifications for every job: 1 day before and 1 hour before.

3C. The "Leave Time" Formula

Never decide when to leave by feel. Use this:

Leave time = Arrival time minus drive time (from Google Maps) minus 15 minutes.

Example: Job at 9:00. Google Maps says 25 minutes. 9:00 minus 25 minus 15 = leave at 8:20. Put "LEAVE 8:20" as an alarm the night before. Aim to arrive 5 minutes early and do your smell check in the truck.

3D. How Long Will a Job Take? (Estimate + 25%)

Most people underestimate. Always take your guess and add 25%. A 2-hour guess is really 2.5 hours. Put the longer time in the calendar.

Between jobs, block: drive time + 30 minutes for parts runs, lunch, and surprises.

Max 3 jobs per day until you've done this for a few months. Max 2 if any job is half-day.

3G. Focus Tricks

  • Write it down immediately. Use one "inbox" note on your phone called "TO DO". Any task that pops in your head goes there. Empty it every evening into the calendar.
  • 2-minute rule: If it takes less than 2 minutes (reply to a text, send a review link), do it now.
  • Hardest thing first. Do the task you're dreading first thing. The rest of the day gets easier.
  • Timer trick: For admin work, set a 25-minute timer. Work until it rings. 5-minute break. Repeat.
  • Social media scrolling is not marketing. Post, reply, get out. Set a 15-minute timer when you open the apps.

4. Getting Customers: Step-by-Step for Every Channel

Channel 1: People You Already Know (do this first, this week)

  1. Open your phone contacts. Write down 50 people who live in or near Austin.
  2. Text each one personally (not a group text). Use their name:
    "Hey [Name]! I officially started my handyman business, ATX Total Properties. I do honey-do lists: TV mounting, shelves, drywall, caulking, doors, power washing, all that. If you or anyone you know has a list, I'd really appreciate the work. Here's my number: (512) 262-8214."
  3. Do 10 per day for 5 days.
  4. Anyone who replies "I'll keep you in mind," reply: "Thanks! Even one small thing helps me get reviews going."
  5. Do a few jobs for friends and family (charge them, maybe with a small discount). Ask every one of them for a Google review.

Channel 2: Google Business Profile (most important long term)

See Section 5 for setup. Then every week:

  1. Upload 3 new job photos.
  2. Write 1 short post (copy Monday's social post).
  3. Ask every customer for a review (goal: 25 reviews in 90 days, 50 in 6 months).
  4. Reply to every review by name within 24 hours.

Channel 3: Reviews and Referrals

  1. Ask for a review at the end of every job (script in SOP 7 of the SOPs doc).
  2. Text the link within 1 hour. If no review in 3 days, send one friendly reminder.
  3. Referral offer: $25 off their next visit for every friend who books. Mention it on every invoice.
  4. Keep a stack of business cards. Give the happy customer 3 cards: "In case a neighbor asks who did this."

Channel 4: Nextdoor (bring it back to life)

  1. Post on Nextdoor once a week, every week. Put it on the calendar (Thursday is a good day).
  2. Alternate between: a helpful tip, a before/after photo, a "this week I have openings Tuesday and Thursday" post.
  3. Check Nextdoor every day at lunch for "Can anyone recommend a handyman?" posts. Reply fast and kindly:
    "Hi [Name], I'm Brandon with ATX Total Properties, a local handyman. I'd be happy to help. Feel free to message me or text (512) 262-8214 with photos."
  4. Ask happy customers in the area to "recommend" your business on Nextdoor. Recommendations make you show up more.

Channel 5: Facebook

  1. Set up a personal profile and a business page (Section 5).
  2. Post 3 times per week using the Content Plan doc (captions already written).
  3. Join 5 to 10 Austin-area groups: your neighborhood, "Austin Moms," "Round Rock Community," "Pflugerville Neighbors," "Cedar Park Community," "Austin Homeowners," etc.
  4. Read each group's rules. Many only allow business posts on certain days. Follow them exactly or you'll get kicked out.
  5. Every day, search each group for "handyman" and reply to new requests within an hour.
  6. Share every business page post to your personal profile. Your friends are your first fans.

Channel 6: Instagram

  1. Post the same 3 posts per week as Facebook (Meta Business Suite posts to both at once).
  2. Before and after videos (Reels) get the most views. 10 to 20 seconds. No talking needed, just add a trending song.
  3. Follow 20 local Austin accounts per week: realtors, neighborhood pages, local coffee shops, property managers.

Channel 7: Neighborhood Blitz ("5 Around" method)

When you finish a job, the neighbors are your best next customers. They saw your truck.

  1. Order door hangers (Vistaprint or similar). Message: "Your neighbor just had their honey-do list done. Got a list? Text (512) 262-8214."
  2. After every job, hang door hangers on 5 houses on each side and 10 across the street (20 total). Takes 15 minutes. Don't put anything in mailboxes (that's illegal). Hang on the door handle.
  3. Ask the customer: "Mind if I put a small yard sign out for a week?" Pick it up 7 days later.

Channel 8: Realtors and Property Managers (steady repeat work)

  1. Google "real estate office near me" and "property management Austin." Make a list of 20.
  2. Every Tuesday, visit 2 offices. Bring business cards and a box of donuts or cookies.
  3. Script: "Hi, I'm Brandon with ATX Total Properties. I'm a local handyman. I do pre-listing punch lists and inspection repair lists, fast, with before and after photos. Can I leave some cards for your agents?"
  4. Get the name of the office manager. Follow up with a text or email 1 week later.
  5. When an agent sends a job, be the fastest and most reliable person they've ever used. Agents send the same handyman work for years.

Channel 9: Referral Partners (other trades)

Other pros get asked for handyman help all the time. Become their go-to.

  • House cleaners, plumbers, electricians, painters, pest control, HVAC techs, carpet cleaners, landscapers
  • Find 2 or 3 local ones on Google. Text or call: "I'm a local handyman. I send people to [plumbers] when it needs a license. Would you be open to swapping referrals?"
  • Send them work first. They'll return the favor.

Channel 10: Lead Apps (optional, use early to fill gaps)

  • Thumbtack and Angi: you pay per lead (often $15 to $60). Reply within minutes or you lose to others.
  • TaskRabbit: takes a cut, but brings small jobs like assembly and TV mounting.
  • Set a monthly budget (example: $200). Track: how many leads, how many booked, how much you made. Stop if it loses money.
  • Always ask app customers for a Google review too, and give them your direct number for next time.

Channel 11: The Truck and Shirts (always on)

  • Magnets on both doors with the badge, "Honey-Do List Specialists," and the phone number.
  • Branded shirt every work day. People will ask at the hardware store. Have cards in your wallet.

6. Pricing SOP: How to Price Any Job

6A. The Formula

  1. List every item on the customer's list.
  2. Estimate time for each item using the chart below.
  3. Add 25% (setup, cleanup, surprises).
  4. Round up to the right package:
    Up to 2 hrs = Quick Fix $190  |  Up to 4 hrs = Half-Day $360  |  Up to 8 hrs = Full-Day $680  |  Extra hours $95/hr
  5. Add materials if you're buying them: cost + 15%.
  6. Send the price in writing the same day.

Example: TV mount (1.5) + 4 pictures (1) + caulk tub (1.5) = 4 hrs. Add 25% = 5 hrs. Half-Day ($360) + 1 extra hour ($95) = $455. Keep it simple: package pricing for lists, flat rates only when it's a single item.

6C. Pricing Rules

  • Never start work without an agreed price.
  • Don't drop your price because someone pushes. Instead, remove items: "To get it under $300, we could do the TV and pictures today and the caulking another time."
  • If someone says "That's too expensive," say: "I understand. My price includes showing up on time, cleaning up, and coming back if anything isn't right. If the budget's tight, let's pick the most important items first."
  • New items added on the job? Price them before doing them: "Sure, that adds about an hour, so $95. Want me to add it?"
  • Raise prices $10/hr when you're booked 2+ weeks ahead. Busy means you're too cheap.
  • Get paid the same day. Big jobs: 50% deposit up front.

8. Slow Day Checklist

No jobs today? A slow day is a marketing day, not a day off. Work through this list from the top:

  1. ☐ Get ready like a job day (hygiene checklist, branded shirt). Mindset matters.
  2. ☐ Text 10 people you know (Channel 1)
  3. ☐ Text 5 past customers: "I have an opening this week. Anything on your list?"
  4. ☐ Search Nextdoor and every Facebook group for "handyman" and reply
  5. ☐ Visit 2 realtor or property manager offices
  6. ☐ Hang 50 door hangers in a nice neighborhood near your last job
  7. ☐ Post a "I have openings this week" post on Facebook, Instagram, Nextdoor
  8. ☐ Upload photos to Google Business Profile
  9. ☐ Follow up on every open quote
  10. ☐ Clean and organize the truck. Restock.
  11. ☐ Learn one skill: watch a YouTube video on a job you'd like to get better at
